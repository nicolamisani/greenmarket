The market

There are 10 markets. Each market is a green product. For each product you write three answers:

  • the sustainability benefit that the company shows;
  • the value added — what the user gets;
  • the value destroyed — what the user loses.

Then you buy Yes or No: you give the probability that the product is still on sale today. You can change an answer until you send all 10. Then you see all 10 results together.

All 10 products came on sale between 2020 and 2026. Five products are still on sale. The names are hidden.

The product attributes

Core value is in the product itself: what it does, or what is in it. Peripheral value is its size, pack, or look.

Value added — what the user gets

Core — performance
The product does the job better than the usual product.
Core — composition
The product has different contents, for example fewer ingredients.
Peripheral — compactness
The product is smaller and lighter. It is easy to carry and to keep.
Peripheral — design
The object looks better, feels better, or is stronger.
Price savings
The product costs the user less money than the usual product.

Value destroyed — what the user loses

Change to the routine
The user must do new tasks. The change is large, small, or none.
Performance uncertainty
The product can fail. The user is not sure that it works each time.
Restricted choice
The company gives fewer types, fewer smells, or fewer sizes.
Price premium
The product costs the user more money. A high price is always value destroyed.

Points

You get points for the probability only. This is the formula:

points = 100 − 400 × (p − r)2

p is your probability, from 0 to 1. r is the result. r is 1 if the product is on sale. r is 0 if it is not on sale. This is the Brier score. It is a proper scoring rule: your best answer is the probability that you believe.

You say 50 percent0 points
You say 90 percent, and the product is on sale+96 points
You say 90 percent, and the product is not on sale−224 points

A safe answer of 50 percent gives you no points. If you are sure and correct, you get many points. If you are sure and wrong, you lose more. Give the probability that you believe. The computer does not give points for your three answers. You compare them with the market at the end.

Your instructor will give you a code of four digits.
